## Cross-source design rules

A recognizable sender is one of the clearest legitimacy cues. Microsoft’s example uses 69525 as the sending number rather than the code itself; Chase lists security short codes 28107, 36640, and 72166; Wells Fargo uses 93557 for enrollment and STOP ALERTS; UPS names 75137 and 24031; USPS uses 28777; VA uses 53079; and SSS says that legitimate SSS messages should come from the branded sender ID rather than an unidentified mobile number. citeturn12view0turn12view5turn13view2turn14view2turn14view9turn14view4turn19view0

A second pattern is “context first, code second.” Real OTP texts are tied to a user action such as sign-in, recovery, registration, device setup, or payment verification, and they usually tell the user to enter the code in the official app or site. They do not ask the user to hand the OTP to a person. PayPal’s reply-based confirmations are a real exception, but even there the interaction is narrow and clearly tied to account activity. citeturn12view1turn12view2turn16search0turn12view3turn13view1turn15view5

For Philippine financial synthetic ham, a no-clickable-link default is especially defensible. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas told supervised financial institutions to remove clickable links in retail SMS and email; Globe says that if a text contains a link, it did not come from Globe; PHLPost says its fake parcel texts ask for address updates and small fees by link; SSS warns about claims or registration texts that push a link; and GCash has shifted many confirmations into the app inbox instead of SMS. citeturn14view8turn15view2turn14view7turn19view0turn12view11

Link-bearing payment texts are therefore the exception, not the baseline. DHL and UPS do support legitimate payment or shipment-management flows, but only with strong shipment-specific context, official sender cues, and brand-specific verification paths. For a synthetic ham set, those families should exist, but at much lower volume than fixed-format OTPs, neutral bank alerts, telecom advisories, or plain delivery-status messages. citeturn14view2turn14view3

Public-service and health reminders should minimize sensitive details. VA says its basic reminders do not include facility or clinic details by default because those details can be protected health information, and the NHS is moving toward simple app-first reminder and invitation messaging with SMS fallback when needed. citeturn14view4turn19view1

## Bottom-line recommendations

The strongest path for safe synthetic ham diversity is to **preserve narrow, nearly fixed wording for the biggest OTP families** and expand diversity across other legitimate service categories. That means keeping Microsoft-, Apple-, Google-, Amazon-, and PayPal-like OTP families small and stable, while using more variety in bank transaction alerts, telecom advisories, delivery updates, device-registration notices, and appointment reminders.
